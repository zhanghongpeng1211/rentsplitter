I Made a Roommate Schedule and It Changed Everything (Even the Bathroom)

By Jake Morrison 2026-06-10 5 min read

The bathroom situation in Apartment 5 was unsustainable. Three people. One bathroom. Everyone needed to leave for work between 7:30 and 8:30am. The bathroom was occupied from 7:15 to 8:45 every single morning, and someone was always angry.

I tried getting up earlier. I tried getting up later. I tried showering at night. Nothing worked because the problem wasn't me — it was that three people were trying to use one bathroom during the same 90-minute window with no coordination.

One Sunday, I made a Google Calendar called "Apartment 5 Bathroom." I shared it with Sarah and Tom. I put 30-minute blocks: 7:00-7:30 Jake, 7:30-8:00 Sarah, 8:00-8:30 Tom. I expected resistance. I expected "this is too structured." I expected "we're adults, we don't need a bathroom schedule."

What I got was relief. Sarah said "oh thank god, I was tired of rushing." Tom said "this is actually great, I can plan my morning now." Within a week, nobody was angry about the bathroom anymore. Because everyone knew exactly when it was their turn.

We expanded the calendar to include cleaning: "Sunday 2pm — kitchen deep clean (Jake)." "Wednesday 7pm — bathroom wipe-down (Sarah)." "Friday 6pm — vacuum common areas (Tom)." Not because we love schedules, but because knowing who does what and when prevents the "I thought you were going to do it" conversations.

The calendar also had a "guests" section. If someone was having a guest over, they added it to the calendar. Not for permission — just for awareness. "Saturday 7pm — Jake has 2 friends over." This prevented the "why are there strangers in my living room" moments.

I know a shared calendar sounds overly organized. I know it sounds like something a project manager would do. But I'm not a project manager. I'm just someone who got tired of bathroom arguments. And the calendar took 10 minutes to set up and saved us approximately 47 arguments over the course of a year.

If you're in a multi-person, single-bathroom situation right now, try it. Make a calendar. Share it. Give everyone a 30-minute block. It doesn't have to be rigid — if someone's running late, they text the group and swap blocks. The point isn't the schedule. The point is that everyone knows what to expect.

Biscuit, by the way, does not respect the bathroom schedule. He walks in whenever he wants. But he's a cat, so he gets a pass.
