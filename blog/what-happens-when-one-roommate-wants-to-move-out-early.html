What Happens When One Roommate Wants to Move Out Early? (A Survival Guide)

By Jake Morrison 2026-09-10 5 min read

Sarah got a job offer in Seattle. It was a good offer. A 40% raise. The kind of offer you don't turn down. The only problem: our lease had four months left, and all three of us were on it.

This is the roommate nightmare scenario. Someone has a legitimate reason to leave, but the lease says everyone is responsible for the full amount until the end. If Sarah left and Tom and I couldn't cover her $800 share, we'd all be evicted. Including Sarah, who would have an eviction on her record even though she was living in Seattle.

We had a meeting. Not a fight — a meeting. Sunday afternoon, kitchen table, coffee. Here's what we decided:

**Sarah's responsibility:** She had to find a replacement roommate or pay her share until she did. This was in our roommate agreement, which is why I always tell people to get it in writing. She had 30 days.

**The replacement process:** Sarah found three candidates. Tom and I interviewed all of them. We had veto power. Not because we're controlling, but because we'd be living with this person for four months. We picked the second candidate, a grad student named Priya who was quiet, clean, and had references.

**The landlord:** We got written approval from the landlord to add Priya to the lease and remove Sarah. This required a $100 lease amendment fee, which Sarah paid. The landlord met Priya, ran a background check, and approved her within a week.

**The deposit:** Sarah got her $800 deposit back from us (Tom and I covered it), and Priya paid her $800 deposit to us. This was a private transaction. The landlord's deposit stayed the same. Sarah walked away with her money, and Priya was financially committed.

**The timeline:** From "I got a job offer" to "Sarah is officially off the lease" took 19 days. It could have been faster, but we wanted to find the right replacement, not just any replacement.

The key to making this work was that we had a written agreement before the crisis happened. Our roommate agreement said: "If someone needs to leave early, they are responsible for finding a replacement approved by remaining roommates. Until a replacement is found, the leaving roommate continues to pay their share."

Without that agreement, this would have been a disaster. Sarah might have just left. Tom and I might have fought about who should cover her share. The landlord might have evicted all of us. The written agreement gave us a roadmap when emotions were high.

If you're reading this while one of your roommates is talking about leaving early: stay calm. Check your roommate agreement. Give them a reasonable timeline to find a replacement. Interview candidates together. Get landlord approval. And get everything in writing.

Sarah is still in Seattle. She's doing great. Priya stayed for the remaining four months and then renewed for another year. Tom and I are still here. The system worked because we had a system.
