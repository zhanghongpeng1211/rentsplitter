How Much Should You Budget for Move-In Costs? (The Real Numbers)

By Jake Morrison 2026-07-14 5 min read

When I moved into Apartment 8, I budgeted $2,500 for move-in costs. First month's rent ($750) plus security deposit ($750) plus some extra for "stuff." I ended up spending $4,180. The "stuff" was $2,680 more than I expected.

Move-in costs are the hidden tax of renting. Everyone knows about first month's rent and deposit. Almost nobody budgets for the other 15 things that cost money. So I tracked everything for my last three moves. Here's the real breakdown.

**Required costs:** First month's rent ($750). Security deposit ($750). Application fee ($50). Total: $1,550.

**Moving costs:** Truck rental ($120). Boxes and packing supplies ($85). Pizza for friends who helped ($40). Gas for the truck ($35). Total: $280.

**Utility setup:** Internet installation fee ($50). Electricity deposit ($75). Gas connection fee ($30). Water account setup ($25). Total: $180.

**Furniture and household:** New mattress because the old one wouldn't fit through the door ($300). Shower curtain, toilet paper, cleaning supplies ($65). Kitchen basics I didn't own ($120). Total: $485.

**The stuff you forget:** Parking permit for new neighborhood ($60). Changing address on ID ($30). Renter's insurance ($15/month, first month upfront). Random things you need immediately and don't have ($180). Total: $285.

**Grand total: $2,780.** And that's for a relatively cheap apartment with minimal furniture needs. If you're moving to a bigger place or buying real furniture, it's easily $4,000+.

The biggest surprise for me was the "random things you need immediately" category. It's the $8 shower curtain rod, the $12 toilet brush, the $25 set of dishes because you packed yours and can't find the box, the $40 worth of takeout because you have no groceries and no energy to cook. It adds up fast.

My advice: budget 3x your monthly rent for move-in costs. If your rent is $800, save $2,400. You probably won't spend all of it, but you'll spend more than you think. And having extra money on move-in day prevents the "I need to put this on a credit card" panic that I've experienced twice.

Use the moving cost estimator on this site to get a more specific number for your situation. It's not perfect, but it's better than guessing. And guessing is how you end up eating instant noodles for three weeks because you spent your food budget on a shower curtain.
