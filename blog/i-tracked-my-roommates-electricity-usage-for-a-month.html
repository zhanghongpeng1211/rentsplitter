I Tracked My Roommate's Electricity Usage for a Month. The Results Were Shocking.

By Jake Morrison 2026-09-22 5 min read

I'm going to tell you something that makes me sound like a control freak, and I need you to understand that I am, in fact, a control freak. But I'm a control freak with data, and the data was worth it.

I put smart plugs on every major appliance in our apartment. The fridge. The TV. The gaming PC. The space heater. The window AC unit. The coffee maker. Everything that used more than 50 watts. And I tracked it for 30 days.

Why? Because our electricity bill was $198 in August. That's $66 per person for a three-bedroom apartment. And I knew — I just knew — that it wasn't evenly distributed. Someone was using way more than their share.

The results were shocking. Not metaphorically shocking. Actually shocking. Here's the breakdown:

**Dave (my roommate):** 47% of total usage. His gaming PC alone used 312 kWh in 30 days. That's more than our fridge. His window AC unit used 89 kWh. His space heater used 45 kWh. Total: 446 kWh.

**Me:** 28% of total usage. My work setup (laptop, monitor, lights) used 78 kWh. My share of common appliances (fridge, router, etc.) was about 89 kWh. Total: 167 kWh.

**Sarah (other roommate):** 25% of total usage. She was at her office most days and barely used electricity. Total: 149 kWh.

Dave was using almost as much electricity as Sarah and me combined. And we were splitting the bill three ways. Sarah was subsidizing Dave's gaming habit to the tune of about $18 a month. I was subsidizing it by about $12 a month. Over a year, that's $360 that Sarah and I were paying for Dave's electricity.

I showed Dave the data. Not in an accusatory way — I showed him the spreadsheet. "Hey, so I was curious about the electricity bill and I tracked usage for a month. Your gaming PC is using more than our fridge. Want to look at the numbers?"

Dave looked at the spreadsheet for a long time. Then he said, "Huh. I didn't realize it was that much."

He didn't realize because when utilities are split evenly, nobody feels the cost of their individual choices. Dave's gaming PC cost him $31 a month in our split system. If he paid for his actual usage, it would cost him $93. That's a $62 difference that Sarah and I were covering.

We adjusted. We kept the base utilities split evenly (fridge, lights, router — things everyone uses), but Dave agreed to pay an extra $40 a month for his gaming PC and AC usage. Sarah and I each saved $13 a month. Dave paid $40 more. The total bill stayed the same, but the distribution was now fair.

I'm not saying you should put smart plugs on everything. That's excessive. But I am saying that if your electricity bill is consistently high and nobody knows why, a month of tracking can reveal the truth. And the truth is usually that one person has a power-hungry device they don't think about.

Data doesn't lie. Roommates sometimes do, but usually not on purpose. They just don't know. Show them the numbers. Let the numbers be the "bad guy." And then split the bill based on reality, not assumptions.
