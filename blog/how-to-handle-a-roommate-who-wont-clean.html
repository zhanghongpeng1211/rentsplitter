How to Handle a Roommate Who Won't Clean (Without Starting a War)

By Jake Morrison 2026-04-14 5 min read

The dishes had been in the sink for five days. Not my dishes. His dishes. A bowl with what I think used to be cereal. A pan with something that had evolved into a new form of life. Three glasses with varying levels of liquid that were definitely not water.

I stared at the sink for ten minutes. I took a photo and sent it to my friend with the caption "this is my life now." She responded with a GIF of a house on fire, which felt appropriate.

I'd been living with Dave for three months. Dave was great in every way except cleaning. He cooked elaborate meals and left the aftermath for... well, apparently for me, since I was the only other person in the apartment and I have a lower tolerance for mold than he does.

I'd tried passive-aggressive notes. "Please remember to do your dishes :)" with a smiley face that I thought would soften the message but actually made it worse. Dave interpreted the smiley face as "no rush" and left the dishes for another two days.

I'd tried doing his dishes myself. This worked for a week, then I realized I was now his maid and he had no incentive to change. Plus, I was angry about it, and anger is a terrible roommate emotion because it leaks into everything else.

What finally worked was a conversation. Not a note. Not a text. A face-to-face conversation on a Sunday afternoon with coffee. Here's what I said:

"Hey, I want to talk about the kitchen. I'm not mad, but I'm getting frustrated with the dishes situation. I clean up after myself, and I'm ending up cleaning up after both of us. Can we figure out a system that works for both of us?"

Dave looked surprised. Not defensive, just surprised. "Oh. I didn't realize it was bothering you that much. I thought I was getting to them eventually."

"Eventually" was the problem. "Eventually" meant five days. "Eventually" meant I was doing them first. So we made a rule: dishes get done within 24 hours. Not immediately. Not "right after eating." Within 24 hours. That's a reasonable standard that allows for "I'm tired tonight, I'll do them tomorrow morning."

We also added a consequence: if dishes sit for more than 24 hours, the other person can move them to a plastic bin on the counter. Not the sink. The counter. Where they're visible and annoying but not blocking the sink. Dave hated the bin. He started doing his dishes within 12 hours.

The key was that I didn't attack Dave. I didn't say "you're disgusting" or "you never clean." I said "I'm getting frustrated and I want a system." The system — 24 hours, plastic bin — gave us a neutral third party. The rule was the "bad guy," not me.

If you're dealing with a messy roommate right now, try this: have the conversation in person, on a Sunday, with no accusation. Propose a specific system with a specific consequence. Write it down. And if they agree to it and still don't follow it, that's information. That tells you something about how the next year is going to go.
